103 community centresQGIS · Hong Kong

CA6241 group project · City University of Hong Kong. Authors: Chen Xinying, Qu Shiyan, Xu Junyi and Zhang Junming.

Where are everyday services accessible?

Our group developed a spatial diagnostic framework for service diversity, walking accessibility and per-capita equity around 103 community centres in Hong Kong, combining points of interest and demographic data.

The study used 5-, 10- and 15-minute walking isochrones, POI analysis and population statistics. A separate 1 km buffer analysis supported community-level comparisons.

Reported finding · Team result

Coverage varies by service type

The report identified 74 of 103 communities below the study’s Hong Kong-wide per-capita allocation benchmark for healthcare facilities, and 71 of 103 for education facilities.

These are April 2024 coursework findings based on the study’s data and method. They are not a current service audit or a measure of residents’ actual service use.

Read the original results · p. 13

Interpretation and limits

What the measures can support

The framework makes spatial differences visible for discussion about service provision. The report also identifies limits in data coverage, sample representativeness and implementation-cost analysis.

Mapped access and facility counts do not establish perceived accessibility, service quality or actual use. These distinctions are part of the study’s limitations.
